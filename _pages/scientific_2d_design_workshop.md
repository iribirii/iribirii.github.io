After conferences I am often asked how I build **figures, schemes, and renders**. In May 2026 I turned that into a practical session for my group at TUM: a hands-on introduction to **2D vector design for scientific publications**.

We worked in **Affinity Designer**, from the interface and core tools through to a first complete visual workflow. I also shared resources I use regularly — icon libraries, colour-palette tools, and [xyzrender](https://xyzrender.readthedocs.io/) for molecular visuals. As a warm-up we built a light example together: a **coffee-making workflow** that ends in a caffeine molecule, then everyone designed their own schemes.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/activities/scientific-2d-design-workshop/02.jpg" title="Coffee workflow scheme demo" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}
<p class="caption">Demo scheme from the session — a simple coffee workflow in vector form, ending with caffeine. © Iñigo Iribarren</p>

The goal was not a long software course, but enough vector literacy to go from **zero to a proposal- or paper-ready scheme**: hierarchy, consistent icons and arrows, and clean exports. I am considering expanding this into a broader format that also covers **3D (Blender)**, colour theory, and data visualisation.
