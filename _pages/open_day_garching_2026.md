On 3 October 2026 the Research Campus Garching opened its doors for the [Open Day](https://www.tum.de/), and I joined colleagues from the groups of [Prof. Alessio Gagliardi](https://www.ee.cit.tum.de/en/sne/home/) and [Prof. Angela Casini](https://www.ch.nat.tum.de/mbc/home/) at the joint [Munich Data Science Institute (MDSI)](https://www.mdsi.tum.de/) and [relAI](https://www.relai.tum.de/) booth in the TUM School of Computation, Information and Technology.

Together with **Niccolò Radice** and **Felix Böhm**, I helped turn our research on finding materials with desired properties — for example high CO₂ absorption — into something visitors could touch and play with. We invited kids and adults to **hand-build molecular models** from atoms and to look closely at **3D structure models**, so abstract chemistry became something you could hold.

We also ran **interactive games** that mirror how we actually work in computational chemistry: guessing CO₂ absorption sites on different materials, picking where a molecule should attach, and then comparing those choices with predictions from a **trained machine-learning model**. The aim was not a lecture, but a short, concrete conversation about how simulation and ML help design better materials.

It was a full day of questions from families, students, and curious visitors — exactly the kind of public engagement I enjoy alongside research and scientific illustration. A longer report of the MDSI / relAI presence is on the [MDSI news page](https://www.mdsi.tum.de/en/mdsi/latest-info/news/full-text/article/exploring-the-future-of-research-a-day-full-of-discovery-at-the-open-day-garching-2026/).
